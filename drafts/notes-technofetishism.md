Informational computing has always had a fetishismic aspect that scientists and theoreticians did not ignore. It is no surprise that in the lectures for the MIT course "Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs" professor Sussman jokingly wore a genie's hat, and admonished his students to learn arcane signs to command the demon in the machine. In every joke there is an admission of guilt. 

> What makes AI expansion in these extreme terms seem unstoppable is a technological determinism rooted in a fetishism of AI, in which it is seen as embodying a pure computational logic, combined with the naturalization of market relations, which suggests that the new technology will inevitably be subordinated to the interests of capital accumulation. Indeed, it is the advent of AI as a new regime of computational power controlled by monopoly-finance capital that is the emerging matrix of class (and imperial) struggle in our time. (cf. Foster)

We want to illuminate the idea of fetishism as it appears in constructs such as "fetishism of ____".

# Metaphysical subtleties and theological niceties

The concept of fetishism is derived from ethnological investigations about the role of _magic_ in so-called primitive societies: men and women endowed with privileged forms of science and technique were capable of affecting the spirits and the subtle matter, and to transform reality. Observers that contemplate the magic, without the knowledge of its inner workings, are left dumbfounded, astonished by its power, and terrified of its wielder, and deem it phantasmic, out of this world. 

Magic does not disappear in the modern world, erased by scientific rationalism. Rather, it goes through a dialectical _éclosion_, where some of its ritualistic, animistic aspects are shed, while other substantive aspects are preserved: the appeal to truth, the dogmatism of established knowledge, the hypostasis of empirical investigation into a general method.

Fetishism as a magical experience is a familiar bridge to our experiences with new technologies. Modern systems of information are so complex that even for trained professionals, the reason why some action works the way it does often remains hidden under words that deflect from its content: a function f(x) -> y hides in this arrow a whole universe of physical and spiritual operations under an _abstraction_; the differences between radically different systems are leveled by a _transparent execution_; the division of responsibilities for different applications in an architecture imposes the consideration of whole systems as _black boxes_. These and other expressions reveal that the activity itself of programming – which is, in the language of Peter Naur, _theory building_, the synthetic construction of a body of knowledge using algorithms and data structures for representing real entities, _"the reflex of the real world" (cf. Marx), has an inescapable recognition of its negative, the infinite _known unknowns and unknown unknowns_. 

The programmer chooses to separate parts of the implementation, of algorithms and data structure, to not contemplate, by reasons of efficiency. Cryptography is the perfect example: while it is possible to invent from scratch and to write by hand hypercomplex algorithms for hashing and encryption, it is common knowledge that that is a bad idea, and it's better to use any of the industry standard algorithms freely available for every programming language out there. Modern artificial intelligence adds a new dimension to this problem space, as the unknowns are hidden behind blackout blinds, through which it is impossible to gaze: the autonomy of a Large Language Model to construct connections in its own terms means its operations cannot be known, even if we so desire. The only things we have access to are its outputs. In the Faustian contract of traditional programming, in which data structures and algorithms are defined by the programmer, he or she still retains a position of control over the machine. The advent of artificial intelligence makes Marx's analysis of the alienation of the human worker that turns the human in the factory environment 'an appendage of the machine' (cf. Marx, Comm. Man.), much more than a philosophical exercise of materialist inversion of Hegel's dialectics of the slave and the master, a naturalist painting of concrete reality.

# A switch so quick the eye can't see

Fetishism works as an analytical key for a plethora of social dynamics in which _appearance_ (Erscheinung) conceals the _essence_ (Wesen) of things, by means of a sort of mystic transubstantiation of the object, that acquires qualities that have nothing to do with its own concept. It makes things sound less like man-made objects and more like medieval holy relics. We should take care, however, to not pay excessive attention to this magical, fantastic aspect, lest we evoke the demon by calling its name, and end up as victims to a sort of _fetishism of fetishism_. It is much less important to apprehend in the objects of our experience the chimera of a supernatural character, than using this idea as an tool with which to expose real fractures in social dynamics, immanent contradictions in the rational development of capitalist system.

In the critique of political economy, for instance, it is not simply the magical power to enchant the masses of consumers, through tricks of illusionism like branding, advertisement and addictive substances, that Marx's analysis of the _fetishism of commodities_ intends to reveal. Rather, as a starting point of his investigation of the systemic functioning of capitalist production and circulation of commodities, he points out that these are perceived as having an innate attribute, value, which we comprehend as a contitutive quality of the thing itself. No counterspell can undo this binding. Like the magician's cloak that hides the levers that control the trapdoor whence the rabbit appears and disappears, the fetishism of value hides the social relations and productive forces necessary to the production of commodities. 

> A commodity is therefore a mysterious thing, simply because in it the social character of men's labour appears to them as an objective character stamped upon the product of that labour; because the relation of the producers to the sum total of their own labour is presented to them as a social relation, existing not between themselves, but between the products of their labour... There it is a definite social relation between men, that assumes, in their eyes, the fantastic form of a relation between things (cf. Marx, I. I. I. §§4).

Let us shed light on the question with a different example, one that makes this idea of transubstantiation very obvious. The advent of new forces of imperial power and violent disputes for territorial dominion resurrected from the time of the Cold War a political discourse marked by a _nuclear fetishism_. According to it, an artifact whose only concrete content is the power to produce a chain reaction of megatons of atomic discharge is believed to be possessed of spiritual properties, such as _deterrence of aggression_ and _guarantee of national sovereignty_, that have nothing to do with its immanent content. This is a fetishism of power (cf. Harrington).

It will be noticed that an important aspect of fetishistic thought is the logical fallacy of causal reversion, where the points of arrival of the analysis are taken as the points of departure, and vice versa: _"so soon as it steps forth as a commodity, it is changed into something transcendent. It not only stands with its feet on the ground, but, in relation to all other commodities, it stands on its head, and evolves out of its wooden brain grotesque ideas" (cf. Marx). This is patent in Marx's critique of liberal political economy as corollary of his analysis of the fetishism of value. The sleight of hand where social relations are swapped with natural qualities enable the writing of post-hoc laws based on exchange value reified as a thing in itself. Instead of basing the analysis of the wealth of society starting from the manner in which the production of artifacts is organized, viz. the social relations of production and the productive forces through which rough matter is extracted from the earth and transformed into useful goods, the analysis of Smith and his followers takes the commodity as given, ready for sale, and from its behaviour in the free trade, it is possible to derive the "market laws". These determine, in the balance of supply and demand, the prices of commodities, the wages and profits, which in turn determine the volume of production in the next cycle. The price, a contingent apparition of the value of the commodity in the market, is taken as having analytical precedence over the necessary components of production and circulation, namely, labor and capital.

> Les économistes ont une singulière manière de procéder. In n'y a pour eux que deux sortes d'institutions, celles de l'art et celles de la nature. Les insitutions de la feodalité sont des institutions artificielles, celles de la bourgeoisie sont des insitutions naturelles. Ils ressemblent en ceci aux théologiens, qui eux aussi établissent deux sortes de réligions (cf. Marx, Mis. Phil. 1847, p. 113, in Cap. I. I. I. §§4, note 33).

# Imagining knight errantry compatible with all economic forms of society  

What does it mean to describe computer technology in its latest incarnations, Big Tech (2010s) and Artificial Intelligence (2020s), as fetishistic? Here, as in the previous examples, we see something claimed to possess spiritual attributes that far exceed its material content. Computer programs appear to us as having a wonderful capacity to, by themselves, solve problems, tear down old structures and transform society for the better. Systems design substitutes politics as the sphere of mediation of conflicting interests in society: social demands can be satisfied by the development and distribution of an increasing number of programs and devices that solve individual everyday problems, and a network where people are allowed to connect, request, consume. "Solutionism" is the term that describes this fetishism of innovation: "problems are to be dealt with via apps, sensors, and feedback loops – all provided by startups" (cf. Morozov).

This attribution of a power of agency to programs and artifacts deeply influences the way we understand the recent changes in social dynamics. For example, the _platformization_ of all aspects of life, e.g. grocery shopping, art and entertainment, love and sex, is perceived as a behavioral change effected by our handheld devices. In the sphere of work, in turn, the phenomenon of the disappearance of the work shift, and the growing trend of people _always on the clock_, available for work from home, or even from commute, appears also as enabled by the handheld connectivity to the global network. This framing problems does away with any references to political and economic disruptions, such as the dismantling of the welfare state and the dissolution of worker unions, that provided financial health and work stability to labourers and their families. The utter imbalance between labor and capital, characterized by a gigantic accumulation of wealth in financial monopolies, and precarization of life conditions of workers, is stripped down of its objective contents and perverted into the anodyne form of "flexible working hours". _"The very potential for the expansion of disposable labor time (leisure) due to automation, Marx explained in his day, contradicts capital’s incessant need to expand surplus labor time. The system therefore seeks to promote, by way of automation—based on the leverage provided by an expanding industrial reserve army—the increased degradation and material dependence of labor (cf. Foster, §§4)"_. In the absence of a social safety net, workers are left in the constant state of triggered, "clueless guinea pigs" (cf. Morozov), available at all times, else this might be the last; always fearful of a sudden layoff, as unemployment means a catastrophe. 

Then, there is the question of security. We don't have time in these notes to discuss the explanation of how, according to the Marxist analysis of capitalist society, impoverishment of a big portion of society is desirable to the capitalist class as a means of reducing wages and increasing profits, and how it exposes the hypocrisy of _fighting crime_. Instead, we just add a few concluding words to the reflection on solutionism as it touches security and artificial intelligence systems. The huge volume of _data_–which, as a representation, is devoid of any concrete class content-and the speed of transmission enable the creation of advanced surveillance systems: cameras in all environments, registries of locomotion of all people, during all day. Such systems are perceived as endowed with a special quality, the efficacy in solving and preventing the occurrence of crimes, through profiling, data crossing, and statistical prediction. _"As Oracle CEO Larry Ellison has said, these technologies allow for tracking and targeting everyone all the time. 'Citizens will be on their best behavior, because we’re constantly recording and reporting everything that’s going on. And it’s unimpeachable…because AI is monitoring the video'"_ (cf. Forster). What this reading fails to account for are socioeconomic factors at the root of urban criminality, and how poverty and disenfranchisement are main drivers of increase of levels of violence. Fetishistic solutionism is a treatment that acts on the symptoms, but never at the causes of the disease. By erasing the class differences in society, with the rubber of a fetishism of innovation, we lose sight of the real question of political economy and society: _"It’s about who controls the economy and whether regular people have a say in the decisions that affect them"_ (cf. Taylor, Levin).
