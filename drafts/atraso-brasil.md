# Gotta catch'em up

Industrialization appears in the history of peoples in the XIX and XXth centuries as the _entering in a process_, like the turnkey that starts the engine and gets the car going. It is a marathon, a race where everyone has the chance to win, as long as they have done the necessary physical preparation, for which there is no better routine than the anglo-saxon recipe. Subjects (meaning entrepreneurs, companies, countries) are free to adopt the model they want; that is, any one but socialism, which violates the divine mandate of private property, and protectionism, which offends the good relations between civilized peoples. But before the race starts, they are advised: if you don't follow the training program, you will not be able to finish the race. "De te fabula narratur". As Marx admonished the Germans, so does Marcuse the peoples at the periphery of capitalism: "the transformation of underdeveloped into industrial societies must as quickly as possible discard the pre-technological".

It is in terms of _reaching a stage_ that the industrialization of so-called backwards countries is defined. Marcuse denies the possibility of any development of industrial society outside the norms of capitalism and then-present day communism[^marcuse-colonial][^marcuse-fateful-interdependence]. As the paradigmatic example of Japan in the development of its industrial capitalism in the XIX century shows, the increase of productive output in industry was accompanied by a broader change of social behaviours, political institutions, aesthetics and habits of thought. Appreciation for German metaphysics grew, as noticeable in the "Kyoto School" of philosophers, while the role of "national studies" was removed from institutional and doctrinal function, and relegated to a position of aesthetical validation of a Japaneseness (Yamato-damashii) that served as ideological ornament for the imperial expansion that was not Japanese in essence, just capitalist. Capitalism is a form of thought and organization of production and society that runs over alternative forms (if we don't want to use the word primitive) of life if and only if they contradict its concept, its _impulse for existence_. This complete normative system operates, in _one dimensional_ society and man, at the levels both of subjectivity and objectivity, that is, the cognitive and moral as well as the institutional and economic[^marcuse-self-propelling-produtivity].

[^marcuse-colonial]: "Is there any evidence that the former colonial or semi-colonial areas might adopt a way of industrialization essentially different from capitalism and present-day communism?"

[^marcuse-fateful-interdependence]: "The fateful interdependence of the only two "sovereign" social systems in the contemporary world is expressive of the fact that the conflict between progress and politics, between man and his masters has become total. When capitalism meets the challenge of communism, it meets its own capabilities: spectacular development of all productive forces after the subordination of the private interests in profitability which arrest such development. When communism meets the challenge of capitalism, it too meets its own capabilities: spectacular comforts, liberties, and alleviation of the burden of life. Both systems have these capabilities distorted beyond recognition and, in both cases, the reason is in the last analysis the same–the struggle against a form of life which would dissolve the basis for domination."

[^marcuse-self-propelling-produtivity]: "A population untrained in the values of self-propelling productivity, efficiency, and technological rationality...(and) not yet transformed into a labor force separated from the means of production."

In the periphery of capitalism, in what is now called the Global South, the marvelous accomplishments of advanced industrial society worked as a true sirens' call that charmed economists and philosophers. The frankfurtian critique of abstract vices of "one dimensional society", "instrumental reason", "commodification of art" could not cause fear greater than the allure of the concrete fruits of modern industry, like cars and refrigerators. It became, then, or was confirmed as such, central the problem of _backwardness_. By the 1930s Brazil arrived at the situation with which the Bolsheviks had to contend on the eve of the 1917 Revolution, the imperative of industrial development. When asked what "communism" meant, so did Lenin respond: "Soviet power, plus the electrification of the entire country". But while the soviets had debated whether, in order to improve the productive forces in the former Russian Empire, it was necessary to carry out a bourgeois revolution, or whether the socialists were capable of delivering this historical task, here in Brazil the debate was settled: it was necessary to grow Brazilian capitalism. So the ideology of _desenvolvimentismo_ was born. Socialism, or at least socialist revolution, was not necessary; the promises of capitalism were good enough. These promises were what a man like Getúlio Vargas set out to advance.

But it was not the attachment to superstitions or to a magical and hierarchical morality, to the relics of feudalism, that, as it was the case in western Europe, thwarted the development of capitalism _ultra aequinoxialem_. Quite the opposite, capitalism, marked by the expulsion of rural labourers from the common and small properties, and their transformation into wage labor force, and the conversion of the major part of the production into production oriented by the exchange value to the detriment of the use value of commodities, is the birthmark of colonial enterprises even before the advent of industrial capitalism. (Rephrase: explain the difference between European history transition from feudal (bond of the peasant to the land, mutual obbligations, personal hereditary hierarchies, favour exchangs, transitions into market economy of the bourgeoisie: movable goods and labour, alienable everything. Brazil, what it keeps (favour exhchanges), what it has that is modern (export profit-driven economy)).

By putting the problem of the people as the problem of backwardness, industrial capitalism comes as a perfect solution. The idol of acceleration is the steam engine, and it demands feeding. The instrumental rationality promises to realize a glorious set of promises: it may not be utopia, but, in the absence of that, productive increase and societal control are the next best things. We just need to adjust the clock. To keep the promise of abundance in the modern horizon, the first step was to replace the archaic feudal structures with modern, rationalized structures. In the periphery of global capitalism, this meant cleaning up the remnants of the colonial period, understood as major contributing factors of _backwardness_. In the 1930s, the problem of backwardness in the economic sense was put in terms of overcoming of the agro-export model and becoming an industrialised country, worthy of taking a seat at the concert of nations together with the others that completed the Second Industrial Revolution. In this transition from a primitive, "backwards" agrarian society towards the advanced society, what actually happens is not a transition where one model gives way to the other; rather than coexisting, the latter produces the former, and feeds on it.

## Backwardness in Brazil

Beginning in the 1930s, when the Brazilian Critical Tradition made Brazil into a concept in order to understand its historical development, the problem of backwardness was centrally in the order of the day, precisely because the horizon showed the possibility of, if not socialism, at least of reaching the stage of productive and social development similar to the best parts of European and North American capitalism. The task at hand for the early Brazilian Critical Theory was to point out the failures in the _national formation_ and the necessary forms for its surpassing, in order to put the country on the tracks, in synchrony with the rest of global capitalism.[^sergio-buarque-europa]

If the problem we face is one of igniting acceleration, the first questions to ask are what are the obstacles working against it, and what is the fuel that can be used for energy. To answer the first, Caio Prado Jr. was one of the first to surpass the European theoretical framework of history and political economy which described, as a totality, the formation of capitalism as the takedown of feudal institutions and productive structures. Applied to the Brazilian case, this tradition described the problem of the backwards situation of Brazilian society as the lingering presence of an inability to do away with the remnants of feudalism[^tese-feudal]. The "Brazilian critical tradition"[^brazilian-critical-tradition] was a first attempt at a theoretical conceptualization of Brazil liberated from the metaphysical contents of European philosophy of history that determined the _Weltanschauung_ of thinkers up to this point. The systemic analysis of pre-industrial Brazil revealed a political and economic structure with little to no semblance to feudal Europe, and therefore it was both nonsensical to conceive a theory of Brazilian society rooted in feudal structures. This made it necessary to discover or invent a new concept of Brazil. 

The Formation of Contemporary Brazil describes colonial agriculture as a roll of negatives: it does _not_ use fertilizers, does not reutilize the land, does not use tools other than the hoe, does not know different species and varieties of culture, does not use water as an energy source. The use of rudimentary techniques and tools such as the hoe and shovel, and the absence of more efficient techniques such as the use of the motor energy of water in mills, the expansion of rural frontier using fire (_queimadas_) instead of more efficient methods for reutilizing the land, such as fertilization and crop rotation, are described not as remnants of pre-capitalist modes of living, thinking, and working. (in fact, even techniques known and used in the Byzantine empire of the VIth century and in the Caliphate from the VIIIth century onwards — the watermill, the noria, irrigation works, crop rotation, systematic manuring[^tecnicas-medievais] — would have been beneficial for increasing the productivity in this distant part of the Portuguese Kingdom), which in fact, without counting the native tribes that inhabited the American continent before the arrival of the Europeans, we never had Instead, the rudimentary techniques and the low level of productivity are much better explained as immanent attributes of the organic functioning of the capitalist system as it realizes itself in Brazil, as an enterprise oriented for the production and commerce of commodities for the world market. One-dimensional society oriented by the production of exchange value and accumulation of capital is the birth certificate of Brazilian society.[^caio-prado-dev-agriculture]

The description paints such a portrait of misery in Brazilian agriculture, in a way that it appears almost prehistoric. It enumerates negatively the instruments and techniques that characterize it, because it would be harder to characterize it positively, that is, by what it effectively has. It does not fertilize the land, and therefore it cannot reutilize it; new farm fields have to be opened by burning the brushes and felling the trees; for the fire to feed the sugarcane mills, it does not reutilize the sugarcane bagasse (dregs), but wood from the forest, and the lack of firewood is one of the reasons for the ruin and abandonment of the mills. Not even the manure of animals is used to fertilize the earth, in order to allow its reuse.

Well, it could not have been the lack of technical and scientific knowledge that imposed such precarious conditions in the material instruments and productive forces. Brazil was a part of the Portuguese Kingdom, the realm of skillful European sailors and explorers, who had cultural connections even in the far reaches of the Earth, in Tokugawa Japan, and it is not unreasonable to suppose they had access to knowledge of modern agriculture. Even if many ways of working were learned from the natives, we cannot accept that the precariousness of the rudimentary state of agricultural technology was merely a remnant of the influence of a pre-technological period of culture. 

Three possible causes for the primitive state of tools in the colonial period: the use of slave labour, the lack of interest of the colonial administration in integrating Brazil to the global market, and the incapacity of the productive process to receive the necessary investments for its virtuous reproduction. The first point avented as responsible for such a state of regressive technological development is the inefficient and barbaric use of slave labor, in which the man is reduced to a beast, and the minimal amount of intelligence is applied in the work. Industrialization depends, we saw in the historical example of the development of European capitalism, on mobilization of collective effort, whose expression is the State, providing technical and scientific education. The creation of the public education system to provide that necessary part of the system, dissolves the cost for the business owner in the collective treasury. Slave labor was used exactly because of its low cost, therefore investing in its technical and professional training would unmake any sense of this form of labour expropriation. The search for maximising short term profits, the same principle fetishized by the Austrian thinkers like Mises and Hayek as the driving energy of innovation, works here, by blocking the incentive for investment in the education of the labor force, by kicking away the bases required for technological advancement.

As for the second point, it is not only slave labor which is responsible for the lagging state of technology. The evidence for this is that agriculture and industry were more advanced in other tropical colonies — the French and English Antilles, and above all Saint-Domingue, which by the end of the XVIIIth century was the most productive sugar colony in the world, with hydraulic mills and irrigation works that the Brazilian _engenho_ never built — which used a similar form of labor force, and in some cases a harsher one.[^caio-prado-dev-agriculture-2] What distinguishes the two cases is not the labour regime but the place each colony occupied in the accumulation of the metropolis, which is to say that the explanation was never anthropological or cultural, or a matter of what the colony lacked; rather, it is a matter of what the system required.

Now, more or less 50 years after the decline of the developmentalist project, we see the return of this fallen ideology. But whereas the original movement called for the replacement of the archaic feudal era (bond between the peasant and the land, mutual obligations between lords and subjects), this new era calls for the replacement of ideas from the archaic socialist (social-democratic, welfate state) era: distribution of the collective wealth, environmental sustainability, popular participation in politics.

The expression _desenvolvimentismo_, that until very recently was an old-fashioned relic buried in dusty volumes of political economy from the 60s and 70s, resurfaced with surprising vigour. From a _gauchiste_ position of marxist and keynesian theoreticians, with barely any force in the concrete political sphere, it is now being raised as a question, and maybe even advanced as a project by voices ranging from the left to the right. The professor of economics Bresser Pereira, wrote one of these volumes, a critical summary of the rise and fall of this ideology[^bresser-desenvolvimento-crise], but his economic policy as Minister of Finance after the nightfall of the military government was hardly a New Deal. Likewise, André Lara Resende, another intellectual/statesman and one of the key figures for economic policy at the dawn of neoliberalism in the tropics, now actively raises the flag of keynesian economics against the _ideological straitjacket_ of the economic orthodoxy, viz. austerity measures, high interest rates as a means for lowering inflation, stagnation of state investments, and spends ink and saliva in advocating the need for reinvestment in national industry and a stronger command of economy by the state.

It is a sign of the times that this state capitalist, interventionist ideology is now being claimed, even if hypocritically, by groups that previously espoused the ideology of _anarchocapitalism_, a mix of vulgarized Austrian School political economy and online cult that gained some notoriety in the figure of Javier Milei, the superhero costume-wearing president of Argentina. The _Movimento Brasil Livre_, now running for political offices, abandoned this neofeudal ideology of absolutely free _human action_ of the individual, in favour of a discourse about industrial development and references to a New State, in reference to the dictatorship in Brazil that initialized the industrial capitalist process, _desenvolvimentismo_.

## Critique de la raison dualiste

The error of the developmentalist movement: obsession with the idea of catching up. Critique of dualist reason.

The corollary of the early Theory of Formation was a sort of _dualist reason_ that separates the process of formation of capitalist systems as an opposition of ancient and modern social structures. In the interior of a national singularity, sectors of industry and groups of people live in the modern world: they produce using high-end technology and consume the products recently launched in the market; meanwhile, different, separate sectors and groups live in the old days, using obsolete technology and governing life according to primitive norms and customs. The second one is a world where scientific rationality has not yet taken place. Just as in the individual dimension, so it is in the universal, where advanced nations to which the future has already arrived coexist in the global theatre with those still awaiting.

What in the 1930s appeared as a beautiful promise, by the 1970s morphed into an ugly recognition of reality. Economic and political analysis revealed a decreasing rate of growth in the capitalist powers, and an increasingly antagonistic struggle between the interests of capital accumulation and the expansion of the welfare state. In this new historical scene, the utopian or theological opening of possibilities was closed, and what became clear was not what we were supposed to strive for as a possibility for realizing in society, but rather that these objectives were out of play. The solar horizon desired by the Brazilian _desenvolvimentistas_ now showed a red moon, harbinger of catastrophe. This dynamic of dependence, where one organ feeds on the other, appears more recently in Nancy Fraser's description of _cannibal capitalism_ as an organism of reproduction through a dual mechanism of exploitation and expropriation.

In the process of formation of Brazilian industrial capitalism, the Consolidation of Labour Laws that guarantees basic rights for workers, e.g. minimum wage, vacation, that historically happened as a civilizational step, also reveals itself negatively as a useful instrument of domination for the national bourgeoisie. This is in line with the marcusian argument that the state of comfort in modern industrial society anesthetizes and douses the revolutionary impetus for the radical transformation of social structures. It is a peace treaty that is advantageous for the owners of capital. "The labour laws are part of a set of measures destined to institute a new form of accumulation... the conversion of huge masses of population in 'reserve army' adequate to the reproduction of capital was pertinent and necessary in the perspective of the form of accumulation that was being created or reinforced: it provided the median horizon for the business calculations, liberated from the perfectly competitive market... on the other side, the labor legislation equated by reducing, instead of increasing, the price of labor force. This operation of equating from the base reconverted even specialized workers to the situation of unqualified workers, and prevented the precocious formation of a dual market of labor force... the regulation of labor laws operated the reconversion to a common denominator of all categories that, instead of injuring the accumulation, benefitted it" (Oliveira, C. R. Dualista).

The internal political-economic development of industrial capitalism is marked by a process of expansion of a white middle class. To this group belong so many of the celebrated heroes of Hollywood, Luke Skywalker, Marty McFly. In both physical appearance and in the landscapes that make their _Lebenswelt_, these representations hypostatize the shape of the particular white middle class man as the universal man in modern society. The improvement of his quality of life, however, was not borne from the natural functioning of industrial society: it was the price for the purchase of his support for the system; even where it shows itself most politically and economically violent. In Brazil, for example, this phenomenon appeared in the form of a supposed _intermediary redistribution_[^bresser-comprar-classemedia] of income from the rich to the middle class, in the form of subsidised housing credit and high salaries, qualifying the period as the "economic miracle" – an expression that reveals more the subjective feeling of this urban middle class and the accounting sleight of hand of state bureaucrats than the objective reality of the country[^chico-redistribuicao-intermediaria]. The myth of "economic miracle" happens in the same time of violent wage suppression and expansion of the rural frontier, where workers were subject to barely subsistence-level income and life conditions, hunger and violence. In the United States, the comfort level of the white middle class cannot be thought without its inseparable double of racial segregation and poverty.

The _Critique of Dualist Reason_ smashes the illusion that backwardness and modernity are separate moments in the formation of national and international capitalism, exposing them as simultaneous aspects of capitalism that feed on one another and are equally necessary for the _functioning_ of the capitalist unit. The epigone of the early Theory of National Formation explains the problem of backwardness, the misery, and the failures in developing the national capitalism not by reconstitution of the historical genesis of the ethical character of Lusitanian society and the methods for settling the territory (Sérgio Buarque), or by the mechanism of productive forces and relations of production in the colonial political-economy (Caio Prado), but in a concrete analysis of the present society and economy, in the choices and tactics taken by the Brazilian business class[^fernando-henrique-empresariado] in comparison with other countries. Francisco de Oliveira lifts the blinds and reveals that the real process of capitalist formation constitutes an organic totality, a _unity of opposites_, where the so-called advanced depends on the backwards for its reproduction.

Even the marxist tradition, whose method is rupture and contradiction, not continuity, sees the development of nations in terms of temporal advancement, or _progress_, _arriving somewhere_. The first nations to turn the key and start the industrial machine (paradigmatically, England) arrived first; the others must accelerate their step to enter and advance in this race, where participation is mandatory. Marcuse, when discussing the transition from pre-industrial into modern industrial society describes the need to eliminate the primitive morals and institutions alternative to those of the _one dimensional society_. But what the Critique of Dualist reason reveals, through the study of the perfect case subject for an analysis of the hypothesis of transition from primitive to advanced industrial society, is that the political economy of Brazil is not divided between completed capitalist forms on one side and, on the other, dregs of precapitalist forms that have failed to adapt to the new mode of production — that is, failed to replace norms of thought and labour such as superstition and magical thinking with the technical and scientific rationality of the management of production and society. Instead, the truth revealed is that these dregs are products of the capitalist system: "Pre-industrial economies in Latin America were created by the expansion of the capitalist world as a reserve of primitive accumulation in the global system; in short, _underdevelopment_ is a capitalist formation, and not simply historical".[^oliveira-underdevelopment-formation]

The process of primitive accumulation of capital does not end in the first period of formation or transition to capitalist society, which would then expand by itself. On the contrary, it is part of capitalist expansion, even in its later industrial phase, to repeat and open new forms of primitive accumulation; what Nancy Fraser calls a continuous process of _expropriation_ that runs in parallel with the classic labour exploitation, like two heads of a hydra, constantly feeding itself on others, in the cyclic process of growth of cannibal capitalism (cf. Fraser, "Cannibal Capitalism"). In the periphery of capitalism, such duality between the primitive forms of expropriation and the modern industrial labor exploitation appears in purest form: "The expansion of capitalism in Brazil occurs through the introduction of new relations in the archaic and reproduction of archaic relations in the new" (Oliveira, C. R. Dualista, II).

In the global dimension, the advanced stage of development was and is allowed by the low cost of materials extracted and produced in the old (and current) colonies where the modernity of supposed civilization had not arrived yet. The list is not exotic, it is an inventory of the golden age itself: Malayan rubber and tin, Iranian and Venezuelan oil, Chilean and Congolese copper, Jamaican and Guinean bauxite for the aluminium of the aeroplane and the kitchen, Brazilian iron ore. The atomic bomb that inaugurated the _Pax Americana_ was built with uranium from Shinkolobwe, in the Belgian Congo. The suburban house with the two-car garage, the appliance, the automobile — the whole furniture of the Happy Consciousness — has a bill of materials, and the bill was not paid where the goods were consumed.
